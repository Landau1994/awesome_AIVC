# Foundational Methods References

Primary references for methods and datasets named in notes. Use the linked source version rather than inferring experimental details from a short citation.

## Flow Matching for Generative Modeling

Lipman et al. introduce flow matching as regression on vector fields along probability paths. This is a methods reference, not an AIVC benchmark.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747).

## Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate with Rectified Flow

Liu et al. study rectified flow to connect distributions using straight transport paths and iterative rectification.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003).

## Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis

Esser et al. study rectified-flow transformer models for image generation. Transfer to gene-expression data requires separate evidence.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206).

## Voicebox: Text-Guided Multilingual Universal Speech Generation at Scale

Le et al. describe a speech-generation model based on flow matching. It is a cross-domain example rather than a single-cell method.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15687).

## Norman

Norman et al. use combinatorial CRISPR activation with single-cell readouts to study genetic interactions. The intervention is CRISPRa, and condition counts depend on the processed subset.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax4438).

## sciPlex3

The sci-Plex chemical-perturbation dataset profiles single-cell responses with sample multiplexing; compound, dose, cell-line and split definitions matter.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax6234).

## Wessels et al. (2023)

Wessels et al. introduce CaRPool-seq for multiplexed single-cell characterization of RNA-targeting CRISPR perturbations. It uses Cas13-based RNA perturbation, which should not be mislabeled as DNA knockout. The article appeared online in 2022 and in the 2023 journal volume.

Source: [original reference or official documentation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01705-x).
